# Habitat Grafit

The web client follows Discord’s server, channel, conversation and member navigation. A separate Messages list shares the same conversation pane. On phones both lists use the existing navigation drawer. Keyboard and touch users can open the same channel actions.

[Discord navigation reference](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045138571-Beginner-s-Guide-to-Discord)

The rail (#14171a), navigation (#1d2023) and conversation (#24282b) step from dark to light in a neutral graphite. Jade (#64b99f) marks primary actions and the selected room; it is kept muted so a filled button never shouts. A brighter green (#5fd08f) is reserved for presence and the speaking ring, so "online" and "primary" never read as the same thing. The sidebar skyline and the smaller channel mark share a deep jade night palette. Preserve the server banner and members’ profile colours. Elevation and borders distinguish a menu from its parent surface.

Habitat Grafit Light puts the conversation on white (#ffffff), the navigation on a pale grey-green (#f4f6f6) and the rail one step darker (#e0e5e4). White cards have a fine edge and a soft shadow. Deep jade (#146a55) marks actions, green (#228b3b) marks presence, and a warm horizon sits behind the daytime skyline. Author and role colours are shaded toward graphite for readability. The call stage follows the page palette. Camera and screen-share letterboxes, nameplates and over-video controls keep their dark surfaces in both themes.

[Secondary reference for dark control surfaces](https://styles.refero.design/style/3b6a17f0-3bdf-418c-a95e-0b89e5a8b2f8)

Use the bundled Figtree family for conversation and controls, and Bricolage Grotesque for introductions and titles. Cozy puts a 40px avatar and a 16px gap beside each group. Follow-ups, system notices and the composer text use the same text column, which follows the chosen density. The typing line reserves 16px above the field. The channel title marks the beginning of the loaded history and scrolls away with it. Room and member names use 1rem, with larger type for profile names. A member’s own banner colour supplies the bands on their profile cover. The server menu, composer and call dock use Habitat’s existing icon set. Keep animation brief and respect reduced motion.

The Messages button on the desktop rail shows the native DM list in the sidebar and carries its unread count. In compact it sits beside Find a conversation. Browsing the list leaves the current chat open. Pressing Messages again or choosing the server icon brings back channels. Find a conversation searches both lists. A member’s profile offers the action to start a new DM.

The room header opens the member list for the active channel. Members start at the top of the desktop right sidebar. Invite Users is available in the desktop server menu for members with permission. The compact members drawer holds Members and its close button; joining a text channel's call from a phone is the first item of the channel menu. Account IDs select the artwork for members without profile photos across chat, the roster, profiles and direct conversations. The voice directory groups dedicated voice rooms and temporary voice rooms in the sidebar. Show each participant below their room and outline the speaking member’s avatar in green. A 20px mark aligns the room names. Leave 2px between rows and reserve hairlines for group boundaries. Channel rows are 36px and member rows are 40px, both rising to 44px on coarse pointers. Joining a dedicated room replaces its introduction with a full-height call stage. Closing the stage reveals any earlier message history. During other calls the grid control puts the stage above the conversation. The same member actions stay available in both positions.

The call stage is one grid for everyone in the call, sized so that all tiles fit on one screen without scrolling. A camera fills its owner’s tile, with the name on a dark plate. A screen share takes the large spot and the people move to a single row beneath; clicking a tile or pressing Enter or Space enlarges it. Repeating the action or pressing Escape returns to the grid. A second shared screen waits in that row: the first keeps the large spot until the viewer picks the other, or asks for both with the button at the top of the stage. On a desktop the stage shows the call buttons along its bottom edge on hover or keyboard focus. They mirror the sidebar controls. More opens the call menu from the desktop dock and the compact call bar. Someone joining settles into the stage and into the list under the room, someone leaving fades where they were. When participants join or leave, the other tiles glide into place over 180 milliseconds. Resizing the window updates their positions immediately. Alone in a voice room, the stage says so under the one tile and offers Haven's invite. On a phone notices rise from the bottom, clear of the header. When the row gets crowded the tiles shrink to faces and the names move to the tooltip.

At 901px and above the account and call controls share the left footer. On phones the channel title, search and channel actions share the header. Account settings open on a separate page with a category column on desktop and the native category picker on narrow screens.

Threads align grouped replies and composer text on a shared column at each density. The thread list, search results and pins share row spacing and keyboard focus. Gallery tiles show filenames on hover or focus, and keep them visible on touch. Both media viewers keep the filename and actions together at the top.

Emoji and reaction cells measure 32px with a fine pointer and 40px on touch. On the sign-in card, Login and Register share a segmented control. Link Server and admin recovery live under Other ways to sign in. Confirmations focus Cancel and return focus to the opener when dismissed.

Grafit uses a 2px jade outline for keyboard focus. Disabled controls keep their shape at reduced opacity. A pending action keeps its width and shows a static ellipsis. Field errors sit below the input, share its danger colour and clear when the member edits it.

Tab enters each main list once. Up and Down move between rows, Home and End reach the edges, and the menu key opens actions. Enter opens the conversation or member profile. In message history, Enter opens actions, E edits your own message, R replies, Delete asks for confirmation and Shift+Delete deletes immediately. Escape returns from history to the composer after open surfaces have been dismissed.

Channel, DM, member and message menus put everyday actions first. Hairlines separate organisation and management, and destructive actions come last. Arrow keys, Home, End and initial letters move through available items. Escape returns focus to the opener.

Grafit’s default profile has a 96px cover, an 80px avatar and a 24px display name. The action row includes the member’s DM, permitted call controls and a personal note saved on this browser for the signed-in account. Hover previews omit the controls. A member’s custom profile theme keeps its own presentation.
